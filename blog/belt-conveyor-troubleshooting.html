5 Common Belt Conveyor Problems and How to Fix Them

📅 June 18, 2026 ⌚ 7 min read ✎ Tianyi Engineering Team

Even a well-installed belt conveyor develops faults over time. The good news: the five most costly breakdowns — mis-tracking, slippage, spillage, idler failure, and belt wear — all have clear root causes and fixes you can apply on the floor. Here is the field guide our service engineers use.

In this guide

1. Belt misalignment (mistracking) 2. Belt slippage at the drive pulley 3. Material spillage 4. Idler / roller failure 5. Premature belt wear & edge damage

1. Belt Misalignment (Mistracking)

Mistracking — the belt drifting left or right — is the single most common complaint. Left alone it chews up the belt edges and damages the structure.

  • Cause: Misaligned pulleys or idlers, uneven loading (material landing off-center), build-up of material on rollers, or twisted frame sections.
  • Fix: Use training idlers to nudge the belt back, not brute force. Check that all idlers sit square to the centerline (within ±1.5°). Clear material build-up from return idlers — a single caked roller is enough to pull the belt off line. Make sure the loading chute centers the stream onto the belt.
Tip: At Tianyi we place self-aligning idlers every 10–15 m and at both ends of horizontal curves as standard. If your line is missing them, retrofitting them is the highest-ROI fix for chronic mistracking.

2. Belt Slippage at the Drive Pulley

The belt turns but the load doesn't move — or the drive pulley spins without driving the belt. This wastes energy and scorches the belt.

  • Cause: Insufficient tension, worn or glazed drive-pulley lagging, wet or dusty pulley surface, or simply overloading beyond the design capacity.
  • Fix: Increase take-up tension until slip stops (but avoid over-tensioning, which overloads bearings). Re-lag a worn drive pulley with rubber or ceramic lagging for grip. Keep the pulley face clean. If the problem appears only at peak load, the conveyor is undersized — revisit the capacity calculation rather than forcing it.

3. Material Spillage

Spillage creates housekeeping hazards, windage dust, and lost product. It almost always starts at the loading zone or transfer points.

  • Cause: Worn or missing belt cleaners (carry-back drops off downstream), poorly sealed loading skirts, badly adjusted transfer chutes, or mistracking at the impact zone.
  • Fix: Fit primary + secondary belt scrapers and tune them to the belt. Seal the loading skirt with adjustable rubber barriers close to the belt. Align the transfer chute to match the belt direction and speed. Pair this with a good dust collector at the transfer point.

4. Idler / Roller Failure

A seized or noisy idler is more than an annoyance — a locked roller generates heat that can damage the belt and, in combustible-dust environments, is a real safety risk.

  • Cause: Bearing failure from contamination (dust/water ingress), lack of lubrication, or impact damage at the loading zone.
  • Fix: Use sealed, greased-for-life idlers in dusty zones. Install impact idlers (not standard troughing idlers) under the loading point. Set a scheduled replacement cycle — Tianyi standardized idlers mean you keep a small spare kit (2–3 per size) and swap in minutes.

5. Premature Belt Wear & Edge Damage

Belts should last years. If yours is failing in months, the cause is almost always environmental, not the belt itself.

  • Cause: Wrong belt grade for the material (heat, oil, abrasion, flame), ongoing mistracking (edge rub), trapped tramp metal, or chemical attack.
  • Fix: Match the belt to the duty — heat-resistant, oil-resistant, or flame-retardant compounds matter. Fit a magnetic or tramp-metal separator ahead of the belt. Eliminate the mistracking first; an edge that rubs the frame will never last.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my conveyor belt drift to one side?

Usually from misaligned or worn idlers, an unlevel pulley, or off-center loading. Re-square the idlers, level the pulleys, and center the feed point to correct it.

How do I stop belt slippage at the drive pulley?

Increase belt tension via the take-up, clean oil or dust off the pulley lagging, and check that the drive is not overloaded. Worn lagging should be re-sheeted.

What causes material spillage on a conveyor?

Spillage is typically from overloaded belts, mistracking, or badly sealed transfer points. Reduce load rate, fix tracking, and add skirting seals and scrapers at the loading zone.

When should conveyor idlers be replaced?

Replace idlers when they seize, develop excessive noise, or show shell wear beyond the limit. A failed idler quickly damages the belt, so schedule replacement during planned downtime.

How can I extend belt conveyor service life?

Keep idlers aligned, maintain proper tension, clean the return side, and protect the belt from impact at loading points with lined chutes and impact beds.

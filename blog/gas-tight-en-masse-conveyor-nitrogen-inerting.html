Gas-Tight En Masse Chain Scraper Conveyor for New-Energy Nitrogen Inerting

📅 August 19, 2026 ⌚ 8 min read ✎ Tianyi Engineering Team

New-energy plants handling battery-grade powders, anode graphite, or cathode precursors cannot allow oxygen into the conveying line. A gas-tight en masse chain scraper conveyor with nitrogen inerting is one of the most reliable ways to move these materials while keeping air out. This article explains the design, seals, purge strategy, and a field installation we supplied for exactly this duty.

In this article

1. Why new-energy powders need oxygen-free conveying 2. How nitrogen inerting works in a sealed conveyor 3. Key design features of a gas-tight en masse conveyor 4. Typical new-energy applications 5. Field case: installed unit 6. Specification guidelines 7. Operational benefits

1. Why New-Energy Powders Need Oxygen-Free Conveying

Many materials in the lithium-ion battery and new-energy supply chain are either pyrophoric, moisture-sensitive, or explosive when mixed with air. During conveying, the powder surface is constantly exposed to new interfaces, and any air ingress can trigger:

  • Oxidation and quality loss: Cathode precursors and reduced-metal powders change chemistry when exposed to oxygen, ruining batch consistency.
  • Thermal runaway: Fine carbon, silicon, or lithium-containing materials can self-heat or ignite when oxygen is present.
  • Dust explosion risk: Even materials with low MIE (minimum ignition energy) become safe to convey when the oxygen concentration is held below the limiting oxygen concentration (LOC).

Because of this, the conveyor is not just a transport machine — it is part of the plant's safety barrier.

2. How Nitrogen Inerting Works in a Sealed Conveyor

Nitrogen inerting replaces the air inside the conveyor casing with nitrogen. The goal is to maintain a slightly positive pressure so that any leakage is outward, and to keep oxygen concentration below the LOC for the material being handled.

A typical inerting loop consists of:

  • Sealed casing: All joints, covers, and shaft openings are gasketed to minimize nitrogen loss and air ingress.
  • Nitrogen injection points: Placed at the inlet, discharge, and along the casing to create a controlled purge pattern.
  • Oxygen analyzer: Continuously samples the casing atmosphere and regulates nitrogen flow to maintain the set oxygen level.
  • Pressure relief / vent: Allows safe venting of excess nitrogen to a scrubber or recovery line.
Design principle: The casing must be tight enough that nitrogen consumption stays economical, yet robust enough to survive thermal cycling, vibration, and occasional overpressure events.

3. Key Design Features of a Gas-Tight En Masse Conveyor

The en masse chain scraper conveyor is naturally well-suited to sealing because it moves material in a fully enclosed rectangular trough. To make it truly gas-tight, we focus on four areas:

3.1 Casing construction

Sections are precision-formed and flanged with flat gaskets or O-ring grooves. Bolt spacing is kept close so the flange remains flat under pressure. For corrosive or abrasive powders, contact surfaces are hard-faced or lined.

3.2 Shaft seals

Drive and tail shafts pass through the casing with packed glands, mechanical seals, or nitrogen-purged labyrinth seals. The seal choice depends on pressure, temperature, and powder fineness.

3.3 Chain and flights

The chain runs in a sealed loop, dragging material en masse with minimal turbulence. Flight clearances are set to prevent grinding or compaction while still allowing the chain to run freely.

3.4 Inspection and maintenance

Gas-tight does not mean inaccessible. Sealed access doors with quick-release clamps let operators inspect, clean, or change flights without breaking the casing integrity.

4. Typical New-Energy Applications

  • Cathode precursor powders: NCM, NCA, LFP, and LCO intermediates often require low-oxygen conveying between calciners, mills, and packaging.
  • Anode graphite and silicon-carbon composites: These materials are sensitive to oxidation and must be protected during milling, coating, and transfer.
  • Lithium compounds: Lithium carbonate and hydroxide can react with moisture and CO₂; a nitrogen blanket keeps the atmosphere stable.
  • Recycling black mass: Recovered active materials from spent batteries are mixed and pyrophoric, making inerted conveying essential.

7. Operational Benefits

  • Safety: Oxygen concentration is controlled, reducing fire and explosion risk.
  • Quality: Material chemistry and moisture content remain stable.
  • Efficiency: Fully enclosed system prevents product loss and dust emissions.
  • Compliance: Meets ATEX, NFPA 654, and local fire-safety expectations for inerted solids handling.
  • Flexibility: Can be arranged horizontally, inclined, or vertically to fit plant layout.
